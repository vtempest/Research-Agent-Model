QwkSearch is free to use signed-out. An account adds saved history, your own
model keys, uploads, and the REASON editor.

## 1. Ask a research question

Go to [qwksearch.com](https://qwksearch.com) and type a question rather than
keywords — "what evidence links microplastics to cardiovascular disease?"
works better than "microplastics heart".

QwkSearch runs the query across its enabled search engines, extracts the top
results, and streams back an answer with numbered citations. Every claim links
to the source it came from.

## 2. Read the sources

Click any citation to open the article in the reader. The reader pulls the main
text out of the page — no ads, no nav — and adds an APA citation. PDFs and
YouTube links work too: PDFs are converted to structured HTML, YouTube URLs are
replaced with their transcript.

See [Reading & extraction](/docs/reader) for what each source type supports.

## 3. Ask follow-ups

Under the answer, QwkSearch suggests follow-up questions. You can also ask a
question about a single article without re-running the search — the reader has
its own Q&A box scoped to that one document.

## 4. Choose a model

The model picker covers Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, Llama, DeepSeek and others.
Signed-out you get the shared default; signed in you can add your own provider
keys under **Settings → Providers** and QwkSearch will use them instead, with
no per-request limits of ours.

## 5. Write it up

Open [REASON](/docs/reason-editor), the built-in editor, to turn the research
into a document. Quotes you save while reading land in the document's quote
list, citations keep their source metadata, and the result exports to Google
Docs, Word, Markdown or HTML.
